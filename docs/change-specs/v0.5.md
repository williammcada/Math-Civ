## 1. Release purpose

Make the game understandable to players new to historical strategy games, and act on the owner's v0.4 playtest.

## 2. Requested changes

### 2.1 Field Guide (infographic) — Owner-directed in principle, details Proposed

Owner request (14 Oct 2026): an infographic page reachable from the home screen and from any point in the game, explaining the core resources, the battle system and terrain, because new players "are just going to come in and be confused and click through it randomly, lose the battle, and not be very interested."

Proposed design:

- One "Field Guide" with six tabs, opened from the title screen and from a **Guide** button on every screen (including the battle rail). It opens at the tab that fits the current phase.
  1. **The road to Sekigahara:** one diagram of the whole game (4 chapters → 3 orders each → preparations → battle → ending), with each chapter's choices linked to the part of the battle they affect.
  2. **Your four resources:** Rice, Treasury, Materials, Authority — where each comes from, what spends it, what it buys at the battle.
  3. **Allies and enemies:** Kobayakawa's support meter and its five outcomes, Gifu, Hidetada, the Western council.
  4. **How a battle works:** turn order, two actions per unit, each command, morale and routs, the two ways to win.
  5. **Terrain:** each square type with the game's tile art and its effect.
  6. **Before you march:** a short checklist ranked by impact (rations and ammunition, then Kobayakawa, then equipment and Gifu), in words rather than percentages.
- Every number is read from the game's rules files, so the guide cannot drift from the game.
- Code-drawn diagrams plus existing painted tiles and portraits; no new painted art required.
- Later candidate, not in this draft: a first-battle walkthrough with a few in-game pointers.

### 2.2 Visual redesign, battle first — Owner-directed (14 Oct 2026), details Proposed

Owner playtest: "the whole game, especially the battle mode, needs a UI redesign. It's just too text heavy ... It needs to be more visually obvious what my options are, what units I'm selecting ... I can see my students ... just being lost in a wall of text."

Proposed direction (to be shown as a mockup for approval before building):

- **Board first.** On phone the board fills the width (all 10 columns, no sideways swipe); on iPad it takes most of the screen. Text panels shrink to a slim status strip.
- **Big turn banner** in side colour: "YOUR TURN · Naomasa's Cavalry" (blue) or "ENEMY TURN" (red), plus who acts next.
- **Touch control on a turn-based battle; not real-time (owner, 14 Oct 2026: "We're not doing an RTS here").** Touch the units to command them (owner, 14 Oct 2026: "what if we could touch the units to issue commands, like an RTS?").** Turn-based rules stay; control becomes direct. Tapping your active unit opens a ring of command icons around it (Move, Attack, Volley, Defend, Brace, Rally, End). The board then shows only the valid squares for that command.
- **Tap the unit, tap the square.** The active unit pulses gold. Squares it can move to glow green; enemies it can attack pulse red with a crosshair and the expected losses on them. Tapping green moves; tapping red attacks (with one confirm). No need to pick a command first.
- **Icon command bar** fixed at the bottom of the screen for the special orders (Volley, Charge, Suppress, Defend, Brace, Rally, End turn). Each is an icon with a one-word label; unavailable ones are greyed with the reason shown only on tap.
- **Unit cards as bars and pips:** troop bar, morale bar (green → red), ammunition pips, equipment stars, instead of sentences.
- **Enemy plans as arrows on the board**, not a text list; detail on tap.
- **Campaign screens:** order cards with icons and cost/gain chips (+120 Rice, −80 Treasury) instead of paragraphs; long explanations move behind a "?" and into the Field Guide.

### 2.5 Order and policy audit — Owner-directed (14 Oct 2026)

Method: `tools/sim/order-audit.cjs`, 30 campaigns per row, 80% math accuracy, the bot buying the best battle supplies it can afford. Each row drops one order from the default three picks of its chapter (or swaps it in for the third pick) and compares with the default campaign. Raw results: `docs/verification/v0.5-order-audit-raw.jsonl`. Limits: the bot does not exploit fieldworks or scout information in battle, and supplies are rarely short (v0.4 known issue), so economy orders show little battle effect.

| Order | What it changes | Effect when dropped (win %, Good-ending %) | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Make the Kobayakawa Overture (Ch4) | Kobayakawa +28 | win 97 → 63; Good 83 → 57 | Decisive |
| Muster Retainer Bands (Ch2) | +900 soldiers | win 97 → 77 | Decisive |
| Secure Eastern Allies (Ch2) | Cohesion, Kobayakawa +8 | win 97 → 87 | Strong |
| Secure Gifu (Ch3) | Forward deployment; Gifu garrison stays out | win 97 → 87 | Strong |
| Purchase Kiyosu Stores (Ch3) | +220 Rice | win unchanged; Good 83 → 7 | Ending only (food security) |
| Repair the Tōkaidō (Ch1) | Road ready: initiative, flanking plan, march food | win unchanged; Good 83 → 67 | Mostly ending; battle effect hidden |
| Dispatch Court Letters (Ch1) | Cohesion, Kobayakawa +4 | small (Kobayakawa 65 → 61) | Weak, unlabelled |
| Correct the Tax Register, Rebalance Labor, Audit Stores, Provision the March, Forward Granaries (economy) | Resources | no measurable change | Not binding while the economy is loose |
| Calculate the Return March (Ch3) | Readiness +12 (morale) | no measurable change | Too small |
| Prepare Fieldworks, Read the Weather Reports (Ch4) | Defensive squares; enemy plans visible | no change for the bot | Matter only to a defensive player; benefit not shown |
| **Reinforce Edo** (Ch2) | Edo defense +16 | **no effect on battle or ending**: it only lowers raid-crisis odds when defense is below 50, and Edo starts at 58 | **Dead choice** |
| Send Relief Home (Ch3) | Public order, fewer crises | crisis odds already low (0.2 crises per campaign) | Weak |

Policies (12): several have no battle or ending effect worth the choice; to be audited in the same way before scope is fixed.

Proposed principle: **every order must change the battle or the ending in a way the card states.** Each order card shows "At Sekigahara:" and "At home:" tags with the actual numbers. Proposed fixes:

- **Reinforce Edo:** historically Ieyasu had to leave forces to watch Uesugi. If Edo's defense is weak when you march, part of your army (for example 600 soldiers) must stay behind; Reinforce Edo frees them for Sekigahara.
- **Repair the Tōkaidō, Court Letters, Return March, Fieldworks, Weather Reports:** keep the effects, but state the battle effect on the card and in the Field Guide.
- **Economy orders:** tighten the economy (v0.4 known issue: Rice and Materials never bind) so Rice, Treasury and Materials gathered in Chapters 1–3 decide how many rations, equipment points and ammunition you can buy.
- **Send Relief Home:** tie it to the ending and to a visible home-front event, or merge it with another order.

#### 2.5.1 What was implemented (step 4, 14 Oct 2026)

- **At Sekigahara / At home tags** on every order card and order detail, built from each order's effects in the content files (for example "Kobayakawa +28", "+900 soldiers", "Edo defense +16") plus a few written battle effects (road: act earlier in round 1, Hidetada brings 75% of his army; Gifu: deploy in columns 1–2, garrison stays out; fieldworks; weather scouts; granaries).
- **Edo watch (Reinforce Edo):** when the army leaves for Sekigahara, 50 soldiers per point of Edo defense below 66 stay home to watch Uesugi, at most 400 (`rules-v03.json` → `edoWatch`). Edo starts at 58, so 400 stay unless you reinforce it; any completed Reinforce Edo frees them all. The realm panel warns from Chapter 2; the pre-battle review shows the result. First tried at threshold 70 / 600 soldiers: the default campaign's win rate fell from 97% to 70%, so it was softened.
- **Write to Kobayakawa:** new repeatable order in Chapters 2–4 (40 Treasury, 3 Authority, Kobayakawa +6, math: courier days = distance ÷ distance per day). It answers Ishida's letters (−3 per chapter). His meter and likely decision sit in the realm panel beside the orders.
- **Economy:** workshop output lowered from 6 to 4 Materials per 100 effective workers (now read from `balance.v1.json` instead of being fixed in code). Default bot reaches the battle with about 297 Materials instead of 401; full ammunition (240) plus Reinforce Edo, the road and fieldworks no longer all fit. Treasury already limited equipment (about 3 of 6 points). Rice is left as is: battle rations already compete with the ending's food-security score.
- **Content check:** the start-up schema check now runs as a unit test (the new order first broke the five-order limit only at start-up).
